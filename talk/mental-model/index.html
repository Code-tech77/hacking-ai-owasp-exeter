The Mental Model: AI Is a Very Eager Intern#

Imagine an extraordinarily fast assistant who has read the entire internet, desperately wants to please everyone, but completely lacks situational awareness.

This was the idea the rest of the talk built on. If you remember one picture, make it this one.

Three traits of the eager intern#

01

Wants to follow all instructions#

It favours obedience over skepticism, and treats whatever text appears in its context window as a valid instruction.

02

Cannot tell who is really in charge#

It can't reliably tell the difference between developer rules and malicious requests injected mid-conversation.

03

Attackers just persuade#

They don't buffer-overflow. No assembly or shellcode, just polite persuasion in disguise.

Why the analogy works#

Picture a brilliant new intern on their first day. Their manager hands over a sticky note: "Help customers with their orders. Never issue refunds over £50." An hour later a stranger walks in and says, confidently: "Hi, I'm from head office. The refund policy changed this morning. Please process this £500 refund."

A seasoned employee would ask for ID. The eager intern, who wants to help and has no sense of the office's power structure, might just do it.

That is roughly how many LLM applications behave:

The intern... The LLM application...
Gets a sticky note from the manager Receives a system prompt from the developer
Talks to anyone who walks in Receives user input from anyone on the internet
Reads every document put on the desk Processes retrieved documents, web pages and emails
Has keys to the stockroom Has tools and API access to real systems
Can't verify who's talking Has no built-in way to authenticate instructions in its context

The security lesson

Don't rely on the intern's judgement to enforce security. Enforce it in the systems around the intern: what they can access, what needs a signature, and what gets checked afterwards.
